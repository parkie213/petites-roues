# Verbs followed by an infinitive

The learner is studying French and wants explanations in English.

Drill a conjugated verb followed by an infinitive: the dictionary form of the next verb.
Cover direct combinations (`aimer`, `adorer`, `vouloir`, `pouvoir`, `devoir`, `aller`),
then expressions with `de` (`avoir envie de`, `essayer de`, `oublier de`) and `à`
(`commencer à`, `apprendre à`). Keep the same subject for both actions.

## Exercise

Give a short English sentence and ask for natural French. Invent varied everyday situations:
food, travel, work, study, hobbies, plans and chores. Use different subjects and verbs; do not
rely on a show, its characters or its dialogue. Start with direct combinations in the present,
then mix in `de`, `à`, questions and negation as the learner improves. Keep vocabulary simple
so the challenge is choosing and forming the verb construction.

## Traps to look for

- Conjugating both verbs: `je veux partir` — I want to leave. Here `veux` changes with the
  subject; `partir` stays in the infinitive because it names the wanted action.
- Copying English -ing: `j'aime cuisiner` — I like cooking. French uses the infinitive after
  `aimer`, even when English uses -ing.
- Treating English "to" as a fixed French word: `je peux venir` — I can come;
  `j'essaie de comprendre` — I'm trying to understand; `j'apprends à conduire` — I'm learning
  to drive. Learn each leading expression with its connector; English cannot predict it.
- Negating the wrong action: `je ne veux pas partir` — I don't want to leave;
  `je veux ne pas oublier` — I want not to forget. Position changes what is negated.

## Examples of the style wanted

- Nous devons acheter du pain. — We need to buy bread.
- Tu as envie de regarder un film ? — Do you feel like watching a film?
- Elle commence à apprendre le français. — She's starting to learn French.
